## 📏 Part 3: Grammar Rule — "A" vs "AN"

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In English, before a singular job or occupation noun, we **always** use **a** or **an**.  
> We cannot say: ❌ *"I am doctor."*  
> We must say: ✅ *"I am **a** doctor."*

### The Rule:
1. Use **A** before words that start with a **consonant sound** (b, c, d, f, g, j, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, t, v, w, y, z):
   - **a** teacher
   - **a** doctor
   - **a** nurse
   - **a** chef
   - **a** student
   - **a** driver

2. Use **AN** before words that start with a **vowel sound** (a, e, i, o, u):
   - **an** **e**ngineer
   - **an** **a**rtist
   - **an** **a**ctor
   - **an** **a**ccountant

## 🗣️ Part 4: Conversational Question & Answer Frames

### Asking About Jobs:
- Question 1: *"What do you do?"*  
  Answer: *"I am **a student**."* / *"I am **a driver**."*
- Question 2: *"What is your job?"*  
  Answer: *"I am **an engineer**."*
- Question 3: *"What does he / she do?"*  
  Answer: *"He is **a doctor**."* / *"She is **a teacher**."*

### Connecting to Prepositions of Place (From Last Week!):
- *"Where does a chef work?"* ➔ *"A chef works **in** a restaurant."*
- *"Where does a teacher work?"* ➔ *"A teacher works **in** a school."*
- *"Where does a doctor work?"* ➔ *"A doctor works **in** a hospital."*
