How to do this task Contractions (Short Forms)
Grammar Rule: In English conversations and casual writing, we join words together using an apostrophe ('):
• I am ➔ I'm (e.g. I am Mike ➔ I'm Mike.)
• My name is ➔ My name's (e.g. My name is Gary ➔ My name's Gary.)
  • 1 Read the full sentence above the input box (e.g., I am Mike).
  • 2 Shorten the verb using an apostrophe (').
  • 3 Type the complete short sentence in the box. Remember to keep capital letters (I, names) and end with a full stop (.).
Teacher Lewis's Tip: Click the 🔊 button to hear natural native pronunciation, and click 🎤 Rec to practice saying it yourself!

How to do this task Writing Numbers as Words (Hyphen Rule)
The Hyphen Rule (21 to 99): When writing compound two-word numbers in English, always put a hyphen (-) between the tens and units:
• 24 = twenty-four | 32 = thirty-two | 68 = sixty-eight
• Spelling Alert: forty has no 'u' (4 is four, but 40 is forty)!
  • 1 Look at the number in digits on the card (e.g., 85).
  • 2 Spell it out in English words (e.g., eighty-five).
  • 3 Type your answer in the box. Remember to include the hyphen (-)!
Teacher Lewis's Tip: Both lowercase (twenty-four) and capitalized (Twenty-four) are accepted!

How to do this task Possessive Adjectives (my, your, his, her, its, our, their)
Matching Pronouns to Possessives:
• I ➔ my ("I love my dog.")
• You ➔ your ("This is your book.")
• He ➔ his ("He drives his car.")
• She ➔ her ("She visits her friend.")
• We ➔ our ("This is our house.")
• They ➔ their ("They feed their cats.")

  • 2 Find the incorrect pronoun and replace it with the matching possessive word ("her").
  • 3 Type the complete, corrected sentence into the box: "She loves her cat." (End with a full stop .).
Teacher Lewis's Tip: Always keep the subject pronoun at the start (She loves...) and put the possessive word right before the noun (...her cat).

Click play to listen or use Rec to practice speaking out loud!

How to do this task Don't Confuse: He's vs His & It's vs Its
Key Differences:
• He's = He is (contraction with apostrophe) ➔ "He's an engineer."
• His = Possessive (belongs to him, NO apostrophe) ➔ "His coat is blue."
• It's = It is (contraction with apostrophe) ➔ "It's sunny outside."
• Its = Possessive (belongs to an animal/thing, NO apostrophe) ➔ "The cat washed its paws."
  • 1 Read each sentence and test: Can you replace the word with "He is" or "It is"?
    • If YES ➔ Use He's or It's.
    • If NO (it shows belonging / ownership) ➔ Use His or Its.
  • 2 Rewrite the full corrected sentence in the box, ending with a period (.).
Teacher Lewis's Tip: Possessive words (his, her, its, their) NEVER take an apostrophe in English!

Click play to listen or use Rec to practice speaking out loud!

How to do this task Near vs Far: "This" and "That"
Demonstrative Pronoun Rules:
• THIS: Used for an object that is NEAR you (in your hand or right next to you).
• THAT: Used for an object that is FAR from you (over there, pointing at a distance).
  • 1 Look at the picture and notice the hand position:
    • Is the hand holding / touching the object? ➔ Choose This
    • Is the hand pointing from far away at the object? ➔ Choose That
  • 2 Type This or That into the blank. Remember to capitalize the first letter (T)!
Teacher Lewis's Tip: Close enough to touch = This. Far enough to point = That.
